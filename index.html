Built on real astrophysics

Every orbit, every star color, every sky is computed from published scientific data — NASA Exoplanet Archive, SIMBAD, and the HYG 41,487-star catalog.

Interactive 3D Galaxy Map

Orbit, zoom, and pan through a real-scale map of the nearest star systems. Each star's position, color, and size are derived from astronomical catalogs.

Spacetime Curvature Grid

Watch spacetime bend in real time. A geodesic grid warps around each star and planet based on actual mass data, visualizing general relativity.

First-Person Alien Skies

Stand on the surface of exoplanets and see the sky as it would actually appear — with the correct suns, constellations, and Milky Way from that vantage point.

Warp Travel Between Systems

Travel between star systems with a cinematic warp effect — star streaks accelerate into a light tunnel, flash at peak velocity, then decelerate as the destination fades in.

Time Control

Scrub forward and backward through simulated time. Watch orbits evolve, see planets move, and trigger rare events like the T Coronae Borealis nova — all at your own pace.

Original Soundtrack

10 ambient tracks composed for the app. Each star system zone plays its own randomized soundtrack that loops seamlessly as you explore.

Sky Postcards

Capture and share screenshots of alien skies as postcards. Save them to your photo library or share directly with friends.

5,500+ Exoplanet Catalog

Browse and search over 5,500 confirmed exoplanets from the NASA archive. Filter by habitable zone, view host star data, and visit any world's sky.

Eclipse Chasers

Every total and hybrid solar eclipse from 2026 to 2051, with real path maps, verified city times, safe-viewing gear guides, and reminder notifications.

See the sky from another world

The surface view places you on the ground of any celestial body in the catalog. The sky above is computed in real time from the 41,487-star HYG catalog, repositioned to that world's coordinates.

  • Accurate day/night cycle with atmospheric scattering
  • Pinch to zoom — from wide-angle to telescopic field of view
  • Drag to look around the full 360° sky
  • Time scrubbing shows how the sky changes over months and years

Geodesics you can see

A real-time spacetime grid bends around every massive body in the system. The curvature depth is computed from actual stellar and planetary masses — heavier objects create deeper wells.

  • Up to 16 simultaneous gravity wells rendered per system
  • Heat-mapped grid lines show curvature intensity
  • Toggle on/off from the display settings panel
  • Educational visualization of general relativity

Travel at warp speed

Tap any star on the galaxy map to initiate warp travel. 520 individual star streaks accelerate, cruise, and decelerate in a physically-inspired 1.75-second sequence.

  • Three-phase animation: acceleration, cruise, deceleration
  • Blue-white starlight palette with violet and warm strays
  • Haptic feedback synced to the warp sequence
  • Destination system fades in as you arrive

The neighborhood

Our Sun plus the ten nearest star systems — every one modeled from published astrophysical data.

The Sun (Sol)
0 ly — Home
Our own star and its eight planets. The starting point for every journey.
Alpha Centauri
4.37 ly
Triple star system. Proxima b orbits within its star's habitable zone. A and B orbit each other every 80 years.
Barnard's Star
5.96 ly
The fastest-moving star in our sky, now known to host four tiny rocky worlds confirmed in 2024–2025.
Luhman 16
6.5 ly
Binary brown dwarfs — failed stars too small to sustain fusion, orbiting each other in the dark.
WISE 0855−0714
7.43 ly
A cold sub-brown dwarf, barely warmer than Jupiter. One of the coldest objects ever found outside our solar system.
Wolf 359
7.86 ly
A faint red dwarf, one of the nearest stars to Earth. Famous in science fiction, now explorable in 3D.
Lalande 21185
8.31 ly
A red dwarf with confirmed planetary companions. One of the brightest red dwarfs in our sky.
Sirius
8.6 ly
The brightest star in Earth's night sky. A binary system — the brilliant Sirius A paired with the white dwarf Sirius B.
Luyten 726-8
8.73 ly
A binary red dwarf system. UV Ceti (component B) is the prototype flare star — prone to dramatic brightness eruptions.
Ross 154
9.7 ly
A nearby red dwarf in Sagittarius. An active flare star, relatively young at roughly one billion years old.
Ross 248
10.3 ly
In about 36,000 years, Ross 248 will briefly become the closest star to our Sun as it passes within 3.0 light-years.
