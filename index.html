Geodesics you can see

A real-time spacetime grid bends around every massive body in the system. The curvature depth is computed from actual stellar and planetary masses — heavier objects create deeper wells.

  • Up to 16 simultaneous gravity wells rendered per system
  • Heat-mapped grid lines show curvature intensity
  • Toggle on/off from the display settings panel
  • Educational visualization of general relativity

Watch a nova build in real time

T Coronae Borealis: a white dwarf siphoning hydrogen from its red giant companion, building toward a thermonuclear eruption visible to the naked eye. The last one was in 1946 — the next could happen any day.

  • Accretion disk rendered from mass-transfer physics
  • Tap the NOVA button to trigger the eruption event
  • 2,940 light-years away — the farthest system in the app
  • Time scrub to watch the orbit evolve

See the sky from another world

The surface view places you on any celestial body in the catalog. The sky above is computed in real time from the 41,487-star HYG catalog, repositioned to that world's coordinates. Find Sol — see where home is from 6 light-years away.

  • Constellations shift and distort from the new vantage point
  • "You Are Here" marker shows our Sun's position and magnitude
  • Accurate day/night cycle with atmospheric scattering
  • Pinch to zoom, drag to look around the full 360° sky

Chase every eclipse through 2051

Twenty total and hybrid solar eclipses, each with a real path map, verified city-by-city timings checked against NASA data, and local reminders so you never miss one.

  • Greatest eclipse time, longest totality, and band width
  • Set reminders: one month and one day before
  • Safe-viewing gear guide included
  • If a city is not listed, it is not in the band

Built on real astrophysics

Every orbit, every star color, every sky is computed from published scientific data — NASA Exoplanet Archive, SIMBAD, and the HYG 41,487-star catalog.

Interactive 3D Galaxy Map

Orbit, zoom, and pan through a real-scale map of 28 star systems. Each star's position, color, and size are derived from astronomical catalogs.

Spacetime Curvature Grid

Watch spacetime bend in real time. A geodesic grid warps around each star and planet based on actual mass data, visualizing general relativity.

First-Person Alien Skies

See the sky as it would actually appear from another world — with the correct suns, shifted constellations, and a "You Are Here" marker showing where our Sun sits.

Warp Travel Between Systems

Tap any star on the galaxy map to initiate warp travel. 520 star streaks accelerate into a light tunnel, flash at peak velocity, then decelerate as the destination fades in.

Time Control

Scrub forward and backward through simulated time. Watch orbits evolve, see planets move, and trigger rare events like the T Coronae Borealis nova.

Original Soundtrack

10 ambient tracks composed for the app. Each star system zone plays its own randomized soundtrack that loops seamlessly as you explore.

Eclipse Chasers

Every total and hybrid solar eclipse from 2026 to 2051, with real path maps, verified city times, and reminder notifications.

Scope Matchmaker

Three questions, one honest telescope recommendation. No brand deals — just the setups astronomy clubs point beginners toward. See what each world looks like through real gear.

Sky Postcards

Capture and share screenshots of alien skies as postcards. Save them to your photo library or share directly with friends.

Red Night Mode

Preserve your dark adaptation with a full red-palette night mode. The entire UI shifts to astronomer-friendly red for stargazing sessions.

Other Worlds Catalog

Browse confirmed exoplanets from the NASA archive. Filter by habitable zone, view host star data, and visit any world's sky.

Battery & Accessibility

Battery saver mode, offline-first caching, reduced motion for warp effects, Dynamic Type support, and softer haptics option.

28 star systems. From home to deep space.

Our Sun, the ten nearest neighbors, seven more within 50 light-years, and eleven landmark systems out to 2,940 light-years — every one modeled from published astrophysical data.

Our Solar System
Sol (Our Sun)
0 ly — Home
Our own star and its eight planets, five dwarf planets, the Moon, and three interstellar visitors (1I/'Oumuamua, 2I/Borisov, 3I/ATLAS).
The Ten Nearest Star Systems (4–10 ly)
Alpha Centauri
4.37 ly
Triple star system. Proxima b orbits in the habitable zone. A and B orbit each other every 80 years.
Barnard's Star
5.96 ly
The fastest-moving star in our sky, hosting four tiny rocky worlds confirmed in 2024–2025.
Luhman 16
6.5 ly
Binary brown dwarfs discovered in 2013. Luhman 16 B has mapped weather: patchy silicate clouds.
WISE 0855−0714
7.43 ly
The coldest known object of its kind. Surface temperature ~250 K, with evidence of water-ice clouds.
Wolf 359
7.86 ly
A tiny, violent flare star. Only 11% the Sun's mass, near the minimum for a true star.
Lalande 21185
8.31 ly
The brightest red dwarf in the northern sky, with a confirmed rocky super-Earth.
Sirius
8.66 ly
The brightest star in Earth's night sky. Sirius B packs the Sun's mass into an Earth-sized sphere.
Luyten 726-8
8.79 ly
Home of UV Ceti — the star so twitchy it named an entire class of flare stars.
Ross 154
9.71 ly
A young, active red dwarf that flares roughly once every two days.
Ross 248
10.3 ly
In ~36,000 years, this star will briefly become the closest to our Sun.
Extended Neighborhood (11–49 ly)
Ross 128
11.0 ly
One of the quietest red dwarfs nearby, hosting a temperate Earth-mass world.
Tau Ceti
11.9 ly
The nearest lone Sun-like star. Four candidate super-Earths, two near the habitable zone.
Teegarden's Star
12.5 ly
An ancient red dwarf whose planets b and c are among the most Earth-like ever found.
40 Eridani
16.3 ly
A triple star of three very different suns. Famous in fiction as the home of Vulcan.
GJ 667 C
23.6 ly
A red dwarf in a triple system with a confirmed super-Earth in its habitable zone.
TRAPPIST-1
40.7 ly
Seven Earth-sized worlds around a single tiny star. Three in the habitable zone.
LHS 1140
48.9 ly
A transiting habitable-zone super-Earth that may be a water world. A prime JWST target.
Deep Sky — Landmark Systems (100–2,940 ly)
TOI-700
101 ly
A quiet red dwarf hosting two Earth-size worlds in its habitable zone.
K2-18
124 ly
Center of the 'hycean world' debate. JWST detected methane and CO2 in 2023.
Polaris
433 ly
The North Star. A pulsating Cepheid supergiant in a triple system.
Betelgeuse
498 ly
The doomed red supergiant on Orion's shoulder. ~764× the Sun's radius.
Kepler-186
580 ly
Home of Kepler-186f — the first Earth-size planet in a star's habitable zone.
Mintaka

Orion's Belt (west). A massive multiple system with an eclipsing binary at its heart.
Alnitak
736 ly
Orion's Belt (east). The brightest O-type star visible from Earth.
Kepler-442
1,206 ly
An orange dwarf hosting one of the most promising habitable-zone super-Earths.
Kepler-452
1,800 ly
A Sun-like star famed for 'Earth's cousin' Kepler-452b.
Alnilam
1,977 ly
Orion's Belt (center). A lone blue supergiant, 300,000–500,000× the Sun's luminosity.
T Coronae Borealis
2,940 ly
The 'Blaze Star' — a recurrent nova expected to erupt any year now.
